## The 4-5-6 structure (P2, P1 preface)

**4 global diagnoses**
1. Skeletal discrepancy: VME (vertical maxillary excess), VMD (vertical maxillary deficiency), Class II / III.
2. Short or hyperactive upper lip.
3. Dentoalveolar extrusion (DAE): gingival line concave rather than parallel to horizon. Causes are supereruption without an opposing tooth, attrition/erosion with compensatory eruption, or a developmental cant.
4. Short or long tooth. Short tooth: microdontia, attrition, or APE (altered passive eruption). Long tooth: recession.

**5 CORE questions** (wording from P1 preface)
1. What are the facial proportions and skeletal relationships?
2. What are the length and mobility of the upper lip?
3. What is the relationship between the gingival line and the horizon?
4. What is the length of the maxillary central incisor?
5. Is the CEJ palpable in the gingival sulcus?

**6 tools**
1. Tissue grafting for root coverage. 2. Crown lengthening. 3. Orthodontic intrusion.
4. Orthodontic extrusion. 5. Surgery (orthognathic, extraction, implant). 6. Plastics (plastic surgery, Botox, dermal fillers).

**Treatment by diagnosis (P2)**
| Global diagnosis | Treatment options |
|---|---|
| VME | Orthognathic surgery (Le Fort I impaction) or Botox to mask gingival display |
| VMD | Orthognathic surgery or restorative to increase incisal display |
| Class II / III | Orthodontics and/or orthognathic surgery |
| Short / hyperactive lip | Botox primarily (P4 adds lip repositioning, lip lift, fillers) |
| DAE | Orthodontic intrusion, functional crown lengthening, or restore at increased VDO |
| Short tooth: APE | Esthetic crown lengthening (with ostectomy) |
| Short tooth: microdontia / attrition | Restorative |
| Long tooth | Root coverage grafting |

## GAD form fields (P1 ch 2, Fig 2-1)

Organized outside-in (face, then lips, then teeth); about 5 min; any trained staff member can complete it.
Face height (mid:lower ratio) - lip length (mm) - lip mobility (mm) - dental-facial midline (R/L) -
central incisor exposed in repose (mm) - gingival line to upper lip in full smile (+/- mm) -
distal extent of smile (tooth # R / L) - incisal edges to lower lip (follows smile line / covered by lip / reverse / irregular) -
buccal corridors, negative space (Y/N) - length of maxillary anterior teeth (chart) - tissue levels (chart) -
angle of incisal plane (chart) - posterior occlusal plane (ok / step-up / step-down / cant) -
pathologic wear (Y/N, teeth) - CEJ located (Y/N) - tooth color - alignment (spacing, overlap) - doctor notes.

## Measurement technique (P1 ch 2)

- **Face height**: middle third = soft-tissue glabella to subnasale; lower third = subnasale to soft-tissue menton. Lips and teeth apart, in repose. Recorded as a ratio, e.g. 65:65. If lower > middle: the lower third is ideally 1/3 maxilla (subnasale to commissure line) and 2/3 mandible (commissure line to chin). Interpret against the emotional smile: if the smile is not gummy, there is no problem.
- **Lip length**: subnasale to the inferior border (wet-dry line) of the upper lip, at the midline, in repose.
- **Lip mobility**: incisal edge to lip in full smile minus incisal edge to lip in repose. Book example: 9 mm and 14 mm gives 5 mm. Mathematical alternative: (central length - repose display) + gingival display in smile.
- **Eliciting the smile**: ask the patient to laugh, not smile, because "smile" gets a clenched grimace. Fallback is the "high E" rule. P3 uses "Emma" for repose.
- **CEJ**: explorer in the sulcus. CEJ not palpable means APE by definition (P3, P6).

## Normative values

| Parameter | Normal | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mid:lower face height | 1:1 | P1 ch 2; P4 |
| Lower third split | 1/3 maxilla : 2/3 mandible | P1 ch 2 |
| Upper lip length | F 20-22 mm; M 22-24 mm (M are 1-2 mm longer) | P1 ch 2, P2; Roe et al. per P4 |
| Upper lip mobility | 6-8 mm. More than 8 mm = hyperactive; under 6 mm = hypomobile/guarded | P1, P2, P3 |
| Central incisor display at rest | F 3-4 mm; M 1-2 mm (young adults; display falls with age) | Vig & Brundo 1978 (PMID 349139) via P1 |
| Central incisor length | 10-11 mm | Gillen 1994 (PMID 7802908) via P1 |
| Central incisor W:L | 75-85% (preferred about 78%) | Wolfart via P4; Chu 2007 |
| Gingival display in full smile | 0-2 mm acceptable. Over 2 mm is excessive (P4 citing Robbins); 3 mm or more is a "gummy smile" (Machado per P4) | P4 |
| Gingival line | Parallel to horizon; concave = DAE | P2 |
| Gingival zenith | Centrals slightly distal; laterals and canines centered. Laterals about 1-2 mm coronal to centrals | Chu 2009 (PMID 19368601); P4 |
| Midline deviation | Lay people miss 3-4 mm (Kokich 1999); over 2 mm judged unesthetic (Ferreira) | P4 |
| Incisal plane cant | 2 mm noticed by lay people | Kokich 1999 (PMID 10825866) via P4 |
| Smile arc | Consonant (follows lower lip) / flat / reverse | P1 GAD; Proffit via P4 |
| Esthetic crown lengthening for APE | Osseous crest about 3 mm apical to the CEJ | P4 |

## Update 2026-10-13: the full book was read (chapters 2, 3 and 11)

Chapters 2 (GAD form), 3 (CORE questions) and 11 (sequencing) of the book (P1) were consulted.
This repository contains no text from the book; use your own copy.

**Where the book overrides the earlier notes (and the tool's rules version 2026-10-13b):**
- **VME**: a long lower third *defines* VME (ch 3). Display at rest and in smile is not required; that came from Norris (P3).
  Ch 2 adds that it is no esthetic problem if the smile isn't gummy. **VMD**: treated by maxillary downfracture + BSSO.
- **Class II / III**: screened with the Ricketts E-line. Upper lip 2-4 mm behind, lower lip 0-2 mm behind; lower lip more than 2 mm behind = convex (Class II); in front = concave (Class III).
- **Lip**: there are four global diagnoses: short, long, hypermobile, hypomobile. Norms are for ages 25-30; the lip lengthens 1 mm per decade after 40.
- **DAE**: three diagnoses. Without wear (intrusion or segmental osteotomy); with wear (intrusion plus restoration, or functional crown lengthening, or increased VDO); from aberrant growth or attrition, i.e. a cant (intrusion, or orthognathic surgery or functional crown lengthening).
  Laterals may sit up to 1.5 mm (ch 3) or 2 mm (ch 2) below the canine-central gingival line.
- **APE** needs two things: a tooth short by measurement **and** a CEJ that can't be felt. The CEJ is only probed on short teeth. Passive eruption is complete by age 15-16.
- **Microdontia**: the teeth are *proportionally* smaller and the CEJ is palpable. **Attrition**: incisal anatomy altered (restore if required; occlusal appliance).
- **Central length**: 10-11 mm (ch 3) or 10.0-10.5 mm (ch 2). Lateral about 8.2 mm; canines match the centrals. **W:L 75-80%** (not 75-85%); widths are recorded only on short teeth.
- **Teeth charted**: first premolar to first premolar (#5-#12).
- **Smile**: "gummy" means more than 2 mm of gingiva in full smile; "aged smile" means more than 2 mm of the central covered in full smile.
- **Repose display**: recorded as negative when the lip covers the edge (hold the lip and find the edge with a probe).
- **Midline**: under 2 mm off is acceptable, and **any** midline cant is unattractive. Mandibular midline is not evaluated.
- **Sequencing (ch 11)**: APE + ortho + restorations: esthetic crown lengthening about 6 months before debanding. APE + ortho without restorations: after debanding. APE before restorations: about 6 months of healing before preparation. CTG with Miller III/IV and ortho: graft before ortho or before debanding. Restoring before the CTG only with Miller I/II.

## Current decision rules

The rules the tool applies are listed on its References tab and implemented in `evaluate()` in
`index.html` (see `RULES_VERSION`). Where they differ from anything above, the code is authoritative.

- **Lip length after 40** (rules 2026-10-27a): the book's "1 mm per decade after 40" is applied per full
  decade, so ages 41-49 use the base norm, 50-59 add 1 mm and 60-69 add 2 mm. This reading is the tool's,
  not the book's.
